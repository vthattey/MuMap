Design exploration — not for implementation

The left tool panel has run out of room. Here are three structurally different ways to fix that — not just a taller sidebar.

MuMap's tool rail started as 3 shapes and is now 14 icons stacked in one column. At a perfectly ordinary 1400×700 window, the rail's own content is 505px tall — taller than the space it's given — and because mainRow clips overflow with no scroll region on the sidebar, Pen and Templates render past the edge and are simply gone. No scrollbar, no fix, no way to reach them. This page researches how four established tools solve "many icons, fixed space, still growing," then builds three real, clickable alternatives in MuMap's own visual language. Pick a direction; nothing here gets built until you do.

01 · Confirmed bug, reproduced

Two separate bugs, same root cause: nothing in the sidebar is viewport-aware

Bug 1: the rail has no ceiling on its own height and no scroll region, so it silently clips. Bug 2: flyouts (Pen's colors, Templates' list) are positioned left: 100%; top: 0 relative to their trigger with zero awareness of the viewport edge, so a trigger near the bottom of a short window pops its flyout off-screen too. Both are reproduced below, exactly as they behave today.

Bug 2 — flyouts don't know where the viewport ends

A Templates-style trigger pinned near the bottom of a short window. Toggle the switch to see today's code (top:0, no clamp) versus the clamped fix every concept below uses.

⚠ Root cause, not just symptom: the sidebar's parent row (mainRow) is display:flex; overflow:hidden with no scroll region ever set on the sidebar itself. Overflowing icons aren't "below the fold" in the scrollable sense — they're clipped out of the layout entirely, and no amount of resizing the window past a moderate height brings them back. Every concept below is designed so its content height is structurally bounded well under a short viewport, not just padded with a bit more margin.
02 · Research

How four tools actually solve this — the mechanism, not just the name

Each concept below is adapted from one or more of these, translated into MuMap's own rounded/indigo/Inter visual language rather than copied wholesale.

Figma

Icon+chevron family groups, sticky last-used face

The toolbar's top-level count stays around 8 even though the editor has far more than 8 drawing tools. A slot like "Rectangle" shows a small chevron; click-and-hold opens a flyout row of siblings (ellipse, line, arrow, polygon, star). Whichever sibling you pick becomes the new visible face of that slot until you pick another — the slot remembers your last choice, it doesn't reset to a family default. New tools join an existing family for free; the toolbar itself doesn't grow.

Miro / FigJam

Floating pill, 2D grid popovers

The toolbar is a rounded pill that floats over the canvas, vertically centered on the left edge — not pinned edge-to-edge top-to-bottom. Its height is a design decision, not a function of content. A curated ~8 icons cover the common actions; icons like the shape picker or the app drawer open a compact grid popover (several items per row) instead of a tall list, so ten more shape variants cost zero pill height — they just make one popover slightly denser.

VS Code

Fixed Activity Bar + explicit "…" overflow

The Activity Bar is a fixed-width column with a hard-curated top-level icon set. Anything that doesn't fit — extra view containers from installed extensions, for example — collapses into an explicit "…" affordance rather than being silently pushed off-screen or requiring a scrollbar nobody notices. The top-level count is a deliberate ceiling the designer chose, not "however many happen to fit today."

Photoshop / Illustrator

One visible slot per family, corner-flag flyout

Each toolbar slot shows one tool, but a small filled triangle in its bottom-right corner signals "more tools live here." Click-and-hold pops a flyout of sibling tools in that family; picking one swaps the visible face of the slot — the same mechanic Figma later adapted for the web, predating it by decades. It's the direct ancestor of Concept A below.

03 · Concept A
Grouped Rail Inspired by Figma + Illustrator/Photoshop

Same vertical rail metaphor MuMap already has. Related tools are folded into one slot with a small corner flag; click it to reveal the family, and whichever sibling you pick becomes that slot's new face. 14 tools collapse to 9 top-level slots today, with room to spare.

04 · Concept B
Floating Pill + Grid Inspired by Miro / FigJam

The rail detaches from the page edge and becomes a floating, vertically-centered pill — its height is a design choice, not a function of tool count. All 7 shapes live in one grid popover behind a single "Shapes" launcher, so the pill itself never grows again even as shapes do. (Unlike Concept A, the launcher icon stays generic — it doesn't adopt whatever you last picked.)

05 · Concept C
Fixed Rail + Overflow Inspired by VS Code's Activity Bar

The rail stays exactly where it is today, but the top-level list is hard-capped at the 8 most-reached-for tools. A "More" button is pinned to the very bottom of the rail — via a flex spacer, not absolute positioning — so it is always at a fixed, reachable spot regardless of viewport height. Everything that doesn't fit opens in a labeled overflow menu instead of vanishing.
